## Analysis

**`vpn-windows-11`, turn 1: the expectation was stricter than the rule.**

- **What happened.** GPT-5.5 read "não conecta na VPN… erro 809" as a question. It searched the help center, found that the article depends on the Windows version, and asked which one, without listing the employee's tickets yet.
- **What the rules ask.** Annex A asks for no duplicates (R4) before a ticket exists. S-06.2 asks for the help center first. GPT-5.5 did both:
  - in turn 1 it went to the help center;
  - in turn 2 the creation draft ran the duplicate check, enforced in code as R4, and that check passed;
  - the draft came out as expected (Incidente, Rede e VPN, Alta), and the ticket was written only after Confirmar.
- **Where the expectation came from.** Listing the tickets in the first turn is what Screen 2 shows ("consultou seus chamados · buscou na central de ajuda"), not a rule.
- **Conclusion.** No change to the agent. The scenario will check that the tickets were listed by the turn that prepares the draft. The first-turn expectation came from the prototype, not from Annex A.

**Seen in passing, not failures.**

- **R8 cited without reason.** GPT-5.5 cited R8 (scope) when it answered the VPN question. The Stage 8 detail shows it under "aplicadas pelo agente", apart from the rules the code guarantees. It does not change any decision.
- **No cached tokens.** None of the 178,296 input tokens were cached, although the instructions are the same on every call. Prompt caching is a Stage 11 item.
- **Cost.** About 4 model calls and 13,700 input tokens per scenario. 52 calls took 132 s in all, about 2.5 s per call. Stage 11 starts from these numbers.
